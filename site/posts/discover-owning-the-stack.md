Some features are planned on a roadmap. Others emerge because two components you already built turn out to multiply each other. Discover is the second kind, and it is my favorite proof that Joule's architecture works.

Joule has a Discover feature, in the spirit of what Perplexity does with its feed: a stream of fresh, synthesized content you can browse instead of query. And it exists as the direct fruit of two things we already had. Our LLM agents. And our own search engine.

This article is about the feature, and about the deeper point: some products can only be built by teams that own their whole stack.

## What Discover is

Search answers the questions you already have. Discover surfaces what you did not know to ask.

Instead of arriving with a query, you arrive with curiosity. Discover presents [a feed of topics? stories? briefs?], each one synthesized by our agents from fresh web content in our index. Not a list of links to go read elsewhere. Synthesized pieces: context, sources, and the connections between stories, produced by the same agents that power everything else on the platform.

## Why owning both halves is the whole feature

Here is the part that matters more than the feature itself. Discover is only possible because we own both components it stands on, and each half solves the other's hardest problem.

**The search engine knows what is fresh and what matters.** Because we built our own index with the 80/20 strategy, we are not blind consumers of someone else's ranking. We see what is being indexed, what is new, what real queries are reaching for. The index is not just a retrieval layer. It is a live signal of what is moving on the web. A team renting a search API never sees this signal. They get answers to queries. We get the shape of demand itself.

**The agents know how to turn signal into substance.** Raw freshness is noise. A list of new pages is not a product. Our agents take what the index surfaces and do what agents are actually good at: read, synthesize, contextualize, connect. The same orchestration core, the same workflow engine, the same document generation discipline from the rest of this series, pointed at a new job.

Put the two together and the pipeline almost draws itself. The index detects. The agents digest. Discover delivers. We did not build a Discover team. We connected two components that already existed, and the feature fell out of the architecture.

## The build-out

The feed refreshes [continuously? daily?]. Topic selection runs on [query demand signals from the index? editorial topic lists?]. Each piece is generated through the same self-correcting loop as our documents: structured output, deterministic validation, agents fixing their own failures before anything reaches a user.

## The strategic point

I keep returning to one lesson across this series, and Discover is its cleanest example.

Features live downstream of architecture. A team that rents its search cannot build this, whatever their model budget is, because the raw signal never reaches them. A team with agents but no execution discipline cannot build it either, because a feed full of broken syntheses is worse than no feed. Discover required both halves, already integrated, already deterministic.

This is what platform thinking buys you. Every component you own is not just a feature. It is a factory for features you have not imagined yet. We built a search engine to cut costs. We built agents to do work. The combination gave us a product neither component promised.

There is more sitting in that combination. Discover is the first fruit. It will not be the last.
